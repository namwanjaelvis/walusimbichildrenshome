Brother Marius
As a student from Germany, taking part in this project in Uganda was an incredible and deeply enriching experience. I was lucky to visit the children’s foster care in Kampala through a friend and support the project. Seeing how happy and loving these children are, despite having so little and often no family left, touched me deeply. We cooked together, took photos, laughed, and shared wonderful moments. Everyone was so warm and welcoming. Junior has built something truly amazing for these children, and I deeply admire his dedication and everything he has achieved. This experience will stay with me for a lifetime.
Brother Walusimbi
Volunteer

I grew up in Uganda’s slums as the youngest child and lost my brother to illness at four. My father’s drinking brought violence, forcing my mother to leave with us. We survived by selling sweets, while my father later became homeless. Poverty, family conflict, and Kampala’s streets shaped my childhood.

Grace
Sponsor Me

Grace was found on Kampala’s streets, quietly begging because she had nothing to eat. Her speech was difficult to understand, and she struggled to stand without help. Lonely, vulnerable, and hungry, she faced each day with uncertainty. Her childhood was marked by poverty, isolation, and the desperate search for food.

Cathy & Carol
Sponsor Me

During the 2020 lockdown, sisters Cathy and Carol were sent into the streets to sell tomatoes because their mother could no longer feed five children. Under the sun, they walked hours carrying bags, exhausted and thirsty. Their childhood became a struggle against hunger, poverty, and responsibilities far beyond their years.

Nandawula Asumpta
Sponsor Me

She lost her father as a baby, and in 2019 her mother abandoned her and her sister. Her older sister struggled to protect them as they faced life alone. During the pandemic, their safety was shattered by a neighbor’s abuse. Asumpta’s childhood was shaped by loss, abandonment, fear, and insecurity.

Kevin
Sponsor Me

On 19 August 2020, Kevin wandered alone into Masanafu Bira, frightened and unable to explain where he belonged. He knew only his name. Police searched for relatives for a week, but nobody came forward. His childhood was filled with confusion, uncertainty, and the absence of family or a home ever.

Nalwoga Hikma
Sponsor Me

At seven, Hikma lost her mother and was left with her father. After he remarried and began drinking heavily, her stepmother treated her badly, denying food, schooling while forcing her to carry water containers. She endured neglect, loneliness, hunger, and rejection, sometimes sleeping beneath trees because home meant a day.
